SIGNAL / PRODUCT DESIGN CONCEPT

A clearer path to what’s next.

A job search workspace that brings applications, interview details, and follow-ups into one place.

Discipline
Product design & visual systems
Scope
Application tracking, job details, and follow-ups
Status
Concept with an early browser-based version

The challenge

A job search involves more than sending applications. Each opportunity carries a different status, contact, deadline, and next step. When those details live across tabs, emails, and notes, it can be difficult to see what needs attention.

SIGNAL explores a focused workspace for keeping that information connected. The guiding design question: how might a job seeker understand their pipeline and choose their next action without rebuilding the context each time?

A workspace organized around momentum

The concept moves from a broad view of the search to the details of one opportunity, then to the timing of the next action.

  1. Track

    See applications grouped by stage.

  2. Prepare

    Open an opportunity and review its details.

  3. Follow through

    Review upcoming interviews and follow-ups.

Three decisions that shaped the experience

01 / Make application stages visible

The board groups opportunities into Applied, Screening, Interview, Offer, and Not Moving Forward. Keeping the stages side by side makes the current state of the search visible without opening every application.

Summary counts support a quick scan, while individual cards retain company and role context. The trade-off is density: a long pipeline will need filtering and an accessible list alternative.

02 / Keep the opportunity’s context together

The detail view brings the role, company, application information, and related actions into one place. This gives the job seeker a place to return before an interview or follow-up.

The hierarchy prioritizes the current opportunity over general navigation. Contact, document, and note areas are directions shown in the visual concept; their full interactions remain to be developed.

03 / Connect progress to a next step

A calendar pairs upcoming events with a monthly view. The intent is to help users move from knowing an application’s stage to knowing when something needs to happen.

The next iteration should make action labels, due dates, and overdue states explicit, with text and icons supporting color cues.

A calm visual system for a busy process

A restrained green accent distinguishes selected navigation and key actions. Neutral surfaces, compact cards, and consistent spacing keep the interface organized as the number of applications grows.

The visual direction balances an overview with readable detail. Keyboard access, focus states, contrast, and narrow-screen layouts still need verification.

From concept to an early tool

The existing browser-based first version supports adding applications with a stage, next step, and follow-up date, then reviewing or removing saved entries. Entries are stored in the current browser; editing an existing entry is not yet supported. The richer board, detail, and calendar screens shown above explore the intended product direction beyond that initial tool.

What I would test next

The design has not yet been validated through user testing. A first study would focus on three practical tasks:

  • Find the next action: identify an application that needs a follow-up and explain why.
  • Update an opportunity: move an application to a new stage and check that the change is clear.
  • Prepare for an interview: locate the event, role details, and relevant notes.

I would observe task completion, missed information, and confusion between application status and scheduled events. Those findings would guide changes before adding more features.

Reflection

SIGNAL’s central idea is to make a job search easier to resume. The strongest next step is to test whether this structure helps people act with less searching and re-reading. The current work establishes a visual direction and a starting workflow; measured outcomes are still ahead.
